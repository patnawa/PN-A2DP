# PN-A2DP v0.1.0.38 — experimental prerelease

**Application 0.1.0.38 · custom driver 0.1.0.25 · Windows x64**

Choose **Windows mode** for use without Test Mode. The separate **Test Mode**
package contains a self-signed development kernel driver; it is **not a
Microsoft-signed production release**. It is signed with the same development
certificate as v0.1.0.30, v0.1.0.34 and v0.1.0.36. PN-A2DP applications and installers
currently have no public Authenticode publisher signature and may show a reputation warning.

## What's new

**Speaker delay and lip-sync (driver 0.1.0.25, application 0.1.0.38)**

Video players keep picture and sound together with the delay Windows reports for the
speaker, and that delay includes what the speaker itself reports. The EDIFIER MR5 reports
25.0 ms right after it sets up the stream itself and 364.0 ms later, while a microphone
measured its real delay at **267-340 ms** over 10 stream starts (median about **315 ms**).

- **Impossible reports are ignored.** A report below a per-codec floor (LDAC, AAC and
  aptX HD 80 ms; aptX, SBC and other codecs 40 ms; aptX Low Latency none) is not used; the
  codec's default applies instead (for LDAC 200 ms) and the status says the report was
  ignored.
- **A fixed speaker delay.** **Headset > Speaker delay (lip-sync)**: **Automatic** (the
  default: the speaker's plausible reports, else the codec's default) or **Fixed**, 1 to
  1000 ms, for the codec that is playing. A change applies at once, without reconnecting.
  In a running stream, the delay in the reported position moves toward the new value by at
  most 10 ms per second of playback and never backwards: 364 to 315 ms takes about 5 s, and
  the 200 ms default to 315 ms about 12 s. The next stream starts with the new value. The
  setting changes what Windows tells players about the delay; it does not delay the sound.
- **Measure with microphone.** The speaker plays 8 short chirps over about 13 seconds, a
  microphone records them, and the delay found goes in as a pending Fixed value;
  **Apply changes** saves it. The recording is analysed in memory and not saved. With a
  fixed 315 ms on the MR5, two checks heard the sound 27.4 ms early and 16.8 ms late; with
  the reports it was 24-97 ms early (364 ms) or 268 ms late (25 ms).
- **Status says where the delay comes from:** "364 ms reported by the speaker", "315 ms
  fixed; the speaker reports 364 ms", "200 ms default; the 25 ms report is ignored". The
  Headset page shows the same as a sentence.
- **Command line:** `pna2dpctl set -d N sinkdelay=315 sinkdelay.codec=ldac` (or
  `sinkdelay=auto`), `pna2dpctl measure-delay [-d N] [mic=<name part>] [apply]`;
  `pna2dpctl config` and `status` show the setting and its source; settings export and
  import carry it.
- Presets and **Auto quality + codecs** keep the speaker delay; the footer's **Defaults**
  button resets it to Automatic.

**Quality when the speaker connects by itself (driver 0.1.0.24, in 0.1.0.25)**

- A stream the **receiver** configures (its own SET_CONFIGURATION or RECONFIGURE, for
  example when it reconnects after a power cycle) now uses your saved quality. Before, the
  MR5 reconnecting by itself ran LDAC at SQ, 660 kbps, under an HQ setting; now it runs at
  HQ, 990 kbps. The same applies to the AAC bitrate and CBR / VBR, the SBC bitpool within
  the receiver's range, the aptX LL buffer level, and the starting budget on a Basic
  Rate-only radio.

**Receiver sample rates (application 0.1.0.37, in 0.1.0.38)**

- `pna2dpctl caps` prints the sample rates of every codec a receiver offers, and the Headset
  page decodes them the same way. After the owner enabled the MR5's own 96 kHz setting it
  shows "rates 44.1k, 48k, 96k" for LDAC. The stream still follows the Windows format;
  LDAC at 96 kHz needs a 96 kHz Default Format and a reconnect, and LDAC stays capped at
  990 kbps either way.

The full record, with the measurements, the design and the tests, is in
[speaker delay and lip-sync](https://github.com/patnawa/PN-A2DP/blob/v0.1.0.38/docs/21-speaker-delay-and-lip-sync.md).

## Install or upgrade

- **Stop playback, close the control panel and close browsers before installing.**
  Chromium-based browsers keep an audio stream open even while paused. If audio is
  playing, Setup leaves the old driver loaded until you restart Windows. With playback
  stopped, drivers 0.1.0.24 and 0.1.0.25 installed without a reboot in testing (each over
  the build installed before it; a single-step upgrade from v0.1.0.36 was not tested).
- **Existing settings are kept.** The speaker delay starts at **Automatic** for every
  device, including devices with saved settings.
- **The speaker-started quality fix needs nothing from you:** a speaker that connects by
  itself now gets the quality already saved for it.
- **To measure and set the speaker delay:**
  1. Stop playback in every application and wait a few seconds; the test needs a fresh
     stream.
  2. Use a microphone that records at 16 kHz or more (a USB or wired microphone; a
     Bluetooth headset's microphone often records too low), and allow desktop apps to use
     it (**Windows Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone**).
  3. Put it 10 to 30 cm from the speaker in a quiet room, at a normal listening volume.
  4. In the panel, open **Headset**, choose the microphone under **Speaker delay
     (lip-sync)** and press **Measure with microphone**. The result goes in as a Fixed
     value for the codec that played; press **Apply changes**.
  5. A speaker's delay can change from one stream start to the next (the MR5's by about
     +-30 ms), so measure three to five times and type the middle value into the **Fixed**
     box before applying. From the command line, run `pna2dpctl measure-delay -d N`
     several times, then `pna2dpctl set -d N sinkdelay=<middle value> sinkdelay.codec=<codec>`.
     The result includes the microphone's own timing (up to about 50 ms with the USB headset
     microphone used in testing), so a value 20 to 30 ms below the middle one is a safe
     choice: sound that comes slightly late is noticed less than sound that comes early.
  6. To go back, choose **Automatic** (or `pna2dpctl set -d N sinkdelay=auto`) and apply.
- **Choose the sound format in Windows:** **Sound > your device > Properties >
  Advanced > Default Format**, then close and reopen the applications that play sound.
- For music, try the **Music** listening profile on the **Transport** tab. Keep
  **Balanced** or choose **Low latency** for games and calls.
- While PN-A2DP is installed, a **newly paired** Bluetooth speaker or headphone binds
  to the PN-A2DP driver automatically. Use **Driver > Use Windows mode** to move it
  back to Microsoft's driver.
- If the codec host service restarts during playback (Setup restarts it), the driver
  falls back to SBC and several seconds of audio can drop. The better codec returns
  at the next playback start.
- For the EDIFIER MR5, pair the classic "EDIFIER MR5" entry, not "EDIFIER BLE", which
  carries no audio.

## Test Mode installation

Read the [complete setup and recovery guide](https://github.com/patnawa/PN-A2DP/blob/v0.1.0.38/docs/17-test-mode-release-guide.md)
before changing boot settings. These steps reduce Windows protections and are for
development testing. If device encryption/BitLocker is enabled, prepare the
recovery key and follow the PC's firmware-change procedure.

1. Verify installer/certificate SHA-256 values against `SHA256SUMS.txt`.
2. For **this PN development build**, prepare **Secure Boot OFF** and **Memory
   Integrity OFF**. Use Windows mode if you want to keep the normal protections.
3. In administrator PowerShell, run `bcdedit /set testsigning on`, then **restart
   manually**. The setting must be active in the running session.
4. Inspect `PN-A2DP-Test-Certificate.cer`. Its SHA-1 thumbprint must be
   **`12EE1EAE30E0DEACCFB2870C393D3C148B1E210E`** and it must have no private key.
   This is the same certificate as v0.1.0.30, v0.1.0.34 and v0.1.0.36; if you already
   trust it, you do not need to import it again. Otherwise, if you intend to trust it for
   testing, run these commands from the download folder in administrator PowerShell:

   ```powershell
   certutil -addstore Root .\PN-A2DP-Test-Certificate.cer
   certutil -addstore TrustedPublisher .\PN-A2DP-Test-Certificate.cer
   ```

5. Stop playback, close the control panel and browsers, and launch:

   ```powershell
   .\PN-A2DP-0.1.0.38-testmode-setup.exe --allow-test-driver
   ```

6. Keep the protected Program Files destination and select the receiver(s) to
   switch. After any requested restart, check the Driver requirements, select the
   receiver in Windows Sound, choose its Default Format and use **Check my audio**.

Setup does not enable Test Mode, import trust or restart Windows automatically.
Adding a certificate or accepting an installer warning does not make this driver
load in normal Windows mode. The Memory Integrity-off requirement is specific to
this validated PN development configuration; it is not a universal rule for every
test-signed driver.
[Microsoft Test Mode guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/install/the-testsigning-boot-configuration-option),
[test-certificate guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows-hardware/drivers/install/installing-test-certificates).

**To leave Test Mode:** switch every PN device to **Driver > Use Windows mode**
or uninstall PN-A2DP first. Confirm Windows-driver playback, then run
`bcdedit /set testsigning off` as administrator and restart. Restore Secure Boot,
Memory Integrity and any suspended BitLocker protection. The full guide gives
the exact commands for removing only this development certificate.

## Known limitations

- **One measurement is not exact.** The MR5's delay varies by about +-30 ms between stream
  starts (one 693 ms outlier), so a single measurement can be off by tens of milliseconds;
  measure more than once and type the middle value.
- Every measurement includes the microphone's own timing: up to about 50 ms with the USB
  headset microphone used in testing (a self-check through its earbud measured 51 ms for
  its playback and recording together).
- The microphone measurement was tried on one speaker and one microphone only. A
  microphone that records below 16 kHz is refused. The owner's first panel measurement
  showed no result, which is not explained.
- A fixed speaker delay applies to one codec (or the same value to any codec); the
  speaker's other codecs stay automatic.
- Not tested on hardware: receiver-initiated AAC, aptX and SBC configurations (the MR5
  offers LDAC and SBC; SBC on the MR5 was measured for its delay only); LDAC at 96 kHz; the
  Sony CMT-SBT40D on these drivers; a single-step upgrade from v0.1.0.36; long sessions; sleep/wake; Driver Verifier and HLK; Secure Boot or Memory
  Integrity on.
- Still open from v0.1.0.36: SBC, AAC and aptX through the sample-rate converter; 88.2 /
  96 kHz Windows formats; the 48 to 44.1 kHz switch during playback; pause and resume on a
  converted stream; the auto-start case; a 2.4 GHz interference session; the Low latency
  profile on a receiver; the new-device defaults on a freshly paired receiver; the
  Transport tab's selector and preset list on a real display.
- A Windows format change reaches an application only when it reopens its audio stream;
  some applications (for example Chromium-based browsers) must be closed and reopened.
- The converter does not convert from 32 or 16 kHz, from 176.4 / 192 kHz, or up to
  88.2 / 96 kHz; those cases select another codec or renegotiate, as before.
- Under heavy congestion, dual-channel SBC can fall to its lowest step (bitpool 8,
  about 132 kbps), which sounds distorted.
- No limiter or headroom: 0 dBFS music can clip in the receiver's decoder.
- aptX's own high-frequency overload is codec-inherent.
- A codec-host restart during playback drops several seconds of audio.
- `pna2dpctl trace` and `sbc_capture` need an elevated prompt.
- Also not verified: AAC and aptX-family receivers, multipoint flows, other adapters,
  LDAC independent decoding and production signing. Codec source inclusion does not
  establish patent clearance or product certification.

## Checksums

`SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the SHA-256 of every asset, and `release-manifest.json`
records the application and driver versions, the certificate thumbprint and the
source revision. Compare a download before running it:

```powershell
Get-FileHash .\PN-A2DP-0.1.0.38-testmode-setup.exe -Algorithm SHA256
```

Report the version, receiver/adapter model, Windows Default Format and
reproduction steps when reporting a problem. **Quality > Export diagnostics**
produces an allowlisted local report; review it before sharing. Neither clean
transport counters nor encoder tests prove universal sound quality or receiver
compatibility.
